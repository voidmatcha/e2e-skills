## Token-ratio definition for the CLI-first screen (offline, unfrozen)

Why: the old decision ratio (`decision.cli_first`, also reused by the G screens and the shipping screens) was the model ratio of **arm sums** over the token-complete cells. That is correct on a balanced complete grid but wrong the moment two arms have different numbers of usable cells: reproduced with 4 of F's 24 cells made incomplete, a true 0.75 became 0.625 (x 20/24), so F looked cheaper than it was. The improvement report already used a different definition (per-usable-cell mean), so one report could print two different ratios for the same pair. The completeness gates hide this today (an incomplete comparator or CLI arm makes the screen inconclusive first); the arithmetic itself was still wrong and would bite as soon as a gate is relaxed or another caller reuses it.

Before: `mean over models of (sum of arm-X processed tokens over complete cells) / (sum of arm-Y ...)`, and the same for cached-inclusive tokens.

After: `mean over models of (sum over tasks of the arm-X usable-cell mean) / (sum over tasks of the arm-Y usable-cell mean)` with equal task weights (direct standardization over the scheduled task mix), usable = token-complete, for both the processed and the cached-inclusive ratio. A task with no usable cell in either arm leaves the ratio undefined (the screen is inconclusive; nothing is imputed or zero-filled). `_improvement_ratio` now uses the same formula, so the decision value and the improvement report agree.

What does not change: the 1.10 bound (processed and cached-inclusive, each), the quality margin, every completeness/fidelity gate (an incomplete comparator or CLI arm is still inconclusive), the 18-usable-cell minimum of the improvement report, and the E/B, E/C pipeline rule (`decision_ratio`, 0.80), which keeps its own accounting. On a balanced complete grid the new value equals the old one exactly, so no outcome that the old definition would have produced on such data changes; the revision was made before any data and the freeze binds it.

Sensitivities reported next to the decision value (never used to decide): `sensitivity_legacy_sum_processed_ratio_*` / `..._total_ratio_*` (the old arm-sum ratio) and `sensitivity_geometric_task_processed_ratio_*` (geometric mean of the per-task ratios, which no single heavy task can dominate).

Why this estimator: the quantity that matters for spend is an arithmetic mean cost, and the ratio of arithmetic means is the standard target for cost data (Barber and Thompson on the bootstrap for arithmetic mean costs, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1123516; the ratio of averages approximates the average of ratios increasingly well as samples grow, so the mean-of-ratios form is not needed). A pooled ratio of arm sums is biased when cell counts differ, and the ratio estimator is biased with order 1/n and asymmetric (Cochran, Sampling Techniques, 1977; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratio_estimator), which is why the report uses a task-clustered percentile bootstrap rather than a symmetric interval, and why Fieller's closed-form interval (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fieller%27s_theorem) is not used here (it can be unbounded with so few clusters). Direct standardization with a common task mix is the usual remedy for unequal stratum counts (https://www.stata.com/manuals/svydirectstandardization.pdf). With 6 tasks and 1 to 3 models the clustered bootstrap under-covers (see the pooled-analysis limitation); the point rule remains a development screen.

## Relaxed token completeness gate (offline, unfrozen; owner decision)

Why: with one model and 24 cells per arm, a single infrastructure defect (one cell with an incomplete token record) made `decision.cli_first`, the G screens and the shipping mapping inconclusive for the whole comparison. That would void a Friday-deadline stage-1 result for a reason unrelated to the arms. The owner chose to relax it before the freeze.

Before: any arm cell with an incomplete pipeline token record made the comparison inconclusive (CLI arm F or G: "incomplete cost records"; comparators A, D and E: "incomplete comparator cost records").

After: a comparison needs at least 18 of an arm's 24 cells per model to carry a complete token record (`TOKEN_MIN_USABLE_CELLS = 18`, the same floor as the fidelity and minimum-cell gates). Fewer than 18 usable cells in F, G, A, D or E (as the screen names them) stays inconclusive with "usable n of 24". Cells above the floor with an incomplete record are excluded from every token ratio (the task-standardized means already use usable cells only), never zero-filled or imputed.

Disclosure (mandatory): `token_completeness` (usable, scheduled and excluded cells per model and arm, plus a note) is returned by `cli_first_decision` and `shipping_decision`; `statement_with_disclosure` carries the note whenever a cell was excluded (the machine-readable `statement` is unchanged); every improvement sentence appends "incomplete cells excluded, usable/scheduled" when its arms lost cells.

What does not change: the 1.10 bound, the quality margins (-0.10, and the 0.20 shipping deficit), a task with no usable cell in either arm (ratio undefined, inconclusive), the fidelity, setup and official-tool gates, the E/B and E/C pipeline rule (0.80; its completeness requirement is a separate rule and is untouched), and the improvement report's own 18-cell floor.

Verification: 10 new offline tests (17 usable is inconclusive, 18 is allowed and disclosed, complete grid prints nothing extra, a task lost entirely stays undefined, unbalanced comparators, G and E comparators, shipping, improvement sentences, protocol text) and one rewritten test (one incomplete F cell no longer voids the screen). Mutation checks (floor 17 or 19, strict CLI arms, strict A/D, strict A/E, no disclosure suffix, no improvement counts) are each caught.
