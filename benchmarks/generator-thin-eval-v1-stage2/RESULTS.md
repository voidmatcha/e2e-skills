## Resolved discrepancy: rollout regex against runner classification

The post-hoc report's audit table counted defining-tool use two ways. The runner's classification (the judge, with the `chain_masks_exit` repair of r2) gives 24 of 24 cells for every arm of every model. A separate audit regex over the rollouts gave the same 24 of 24 for arms A, D and E and for `gpt-6-sol` F and G, but lower counts for the CLI arms of two models:

| Model | Arm F regex / runner | Arm G regex / runner |
|---|---|---|
| gpt-6-astra | 11 / 24 | 14 / 24 |
| gpt-6.1-sol | 19 / 24 | 15 / 24 |

Resolved 2026-10-17 (post-hoc, read-only): the regex was wrong, not the runner. Its rule required both an `open` verb and a `close` verb in the cell, so it tested the session lifecycle rather than exploration, and it missed the `close` call whenever the model wrapped CLI commands in a JavaScript loop, in three forms: string concatenation (`... -s=X "+cmd`), a template literal (`... -s=X ${command}`), and an array whose elements are whole commands. In every missed cell the `open` was matched and only the `close` was not. The earlier untested explanation, `bash -c` batching, was not the cause in these rollouts: it appears only in the runner's own compliance command strings, not in the model calls the regex read.

A corrected counter ([`evidence/corrected-counter/corrected_counter.py`](evidence/corrected-counter/corrected_counter.py)) reads only the cell lists in `final.json` and the Codex rollout logs, never the runner classification. It counts the verb after `playwright cli` or `playwright-cli` (skipping `-s=`, `--config` and `--session` flags), also counts verbs taken from the quoted strings of the dynamic forms, and cross-checks against the call outputs (`Page URL:`, `### Page`, `### Snapshot`, `### Ran Playwright code`). Result for the 144 CLI-arm cells (arms F and G, 24 cells each, three models): every cell contains at least one `open`, one `goto`, one `snapshot` and one `close`, no cell has zero exploration calls, and the count agrees with the runner classification in 144 of 144 cells. Exploration calls (`open`, `goto`, `snapshot`) per cell were at least 7, with a median of 12 to 13.5. The 216 official-arm cells (A, D, E) contain no Playwright CLI call, so the arm separation holds, and the CLI arms contain no official MCP browser tool call.

Condition 3 of the adoption sentence therefore rests on a classification that a log-based count reproduces, and the regex counts above should not be cited as tool-use counts. This confirms that the CLI arms used their defining tool; it says nothing about quality or tokens. The counter is itself a regex, so a model that assembled commands in a variable would be missed; the output-side cross-check found no such case in this data. The re-run output is in [`evidence/corrected-counter/counter-output.txt`](evidence/corrected-counter/counter-output.txt) and the per-cell record is `cells.json` in the same folder. The post-hoc report script and `evidence/report3.md` were not modified; `report3.md` still prints the regex column and is superseded on that column by this section.

## Limits

Six synthetic applications and three models from one vendor family; ceiling effects on the CLI arms (many cells at 1.000); the bootstrap resamples six tasks; the pooling crosses two freezes; safety rules are exercised by the cells but not scored by this comparison. The report's older `summary_statement` field still says the sufficiency statement needs reviewer and full-skill comparators; that is the pre-existing `decision.sufficiency` text and is not the adoption rule applied here. This is development evidence, not a release-grade test.
