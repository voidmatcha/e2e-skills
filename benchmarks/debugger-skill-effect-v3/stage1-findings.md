# Stage 1: why the skill text did not beat the taxonomy-free arm in v2

Read-only analysis of [debugger-skill-effect-v2](../debugger-skill-effect-v2/README.md). Evidence: `reports/claude-sonnet.json` (model statements), `reports/claude-sonnet-judged-by-codex-sol.json` (judge scores and notes), `blind-labels/f-code-mapping-ledger.json` (both mappers' codes and reasons), and the 2.0.0 debugger skill text at `ed9489c`. Nothing in v2 was changed.

## 1. The three cases where the arms differed

**`pw-02` (react-aria listbox closed by auto-scroll).** Every arm described the same timeline; the judge gave 2 only when the statement said the scroll *closes* the popover and 1 when it said the listbox *closed after* the scroll. Skill 2,1,1; legend 1,1,1; free 2,1,2. Skill r1 ("closed by ~240 ms, when the modal body scrolled itself") scored 2; skill r2 ("closed at 240 ms, after the modal body auto-scrolled") scored 1 for the same mechanism in temporal rather than causal wording. The skill arm's statements also spent their one sentence on the F14 framing ("raced a transient listbox", "the transient listbox disappears inside the window the click can hit") instead of on what caused the transient. Finding: the output contract asks for a "one-sentence explanation" of root cause but does not say that the sentence must name cause and effect; the F14 row's vocabulary ("transient element removed before it is observed") is a symptom description that the model copies into the root-cause line. The difference is one call per case and partly judge wording-sensitivity; it is not a large effect.

**`pw-13` (virtualised list, non-retrying `isVisible()` inside `expect.poll`).** Skill 2,2,2; free 2,2,2; legend 1,2,2. The skill arm was correct and tied the free arm; only the legend arm lost one call by blaming insufficient scrolling. The skill arm chose F14 in all three calls, while both blind mappers chose F1 (one of them "forced"). The disagreement is about where "content not yet rendered because a probe did not wait" belongs: the F14 row says "content not yet rendered", the F1 row says "TimeoutError, duration near maxTimeout, passes on retry", and neither row says which one covers a non-retrying read of an asynchronously rendering list. Finding: F1 and F14 are defined by different kinds of evidence (symptom pattern against mechanism), so a case that shows both is assignable to either.

**`cy-06` (click at the bounding-box midpoint of a bowtie polygon misses the shape).** Skill 1,1,1 with F5 in all three calls; legend 1,1,1 (F2/F12/F2); free 2,2,1. The free arm twice said the click "lands on an edge or empty area rather than inside a lobe" (scored 2). The skill arm said the click "misses the filled shape" or "lands in the empty gap beside its lobes" and then spent the rest of the sentence on the F5 narrative: "the wrong state (no selection) is never verified before the join". The judge wanted the geometric reason the point is off the shape; the F5 framing ("action completed but wrong state remains") pulled the skill arm toward the missing-verification story and away from the geometry. The judge also scored skill r1 and free r2 differently for near-identical wording, so part of this is grading noise. Finding: the F5 row invites a "missing Then" reading for any action-then-wrong-state failure, including a click that never reached its target, where the useful cause is why the target was missed (F2 territory).

Across all 26 cases the arms were within one call of each other everywhere except these three; 21 cases were a full match for every arm. The free arm's 0.90 is a ceiling, and the artifacts carry the observations the maintainers needed, so the skill text had little to add on this corpus.

## 2. Why the blind mappers disagreed on 11 of 26 real fixes

Ledger: `../debugger-skill-effect-v2/blind-labels/f-code-mapping-ledger.json`.

| Boundary | Disputed cases | What the maintainers' fix was |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F1 vs F14 | `pw-14`, `pw-03`, `pw-02`, `pw-07`, `cy-08`, `pw-18` (6) | a one-shot read of a value that an asynchronous render, transition, debounce, or lazy load had not finished producing; fixed with a retrying wait on the final state |
| F1 vs F11 | `pw-08`, `cy-04` (2) | a UI assertion made before a backend or multi-request load had finished; fixed by waiting for the real completion signal |
| F7 vs F11 | `pw-06` (1) | parallel workers colliding on a shared singleton |
| F5 vs F2 | `cy-06` (1) | a click whose coordinates were off the target shape |
| F6 vs F12 | `pw-11` (1) | a page-object fallback that drove a search box on `about:blank` |

Six of eleven disputes sit on one boundary. The mappers' own reasons show the rule they were missing: mapper A read F14 as "the content had not finished settling within the window the assertion read it" and mapper B read F1 as "fixed with a retrying poll". Both readings are consistent with the 2.0.0 rows because the rows do not say how to split them. Five further agreed labels were marked "forced" by one or both mappers (`pw-10` and `pw-05` waits that never match a request, `cy-05` a mis-scoped exception handler, `cy-03` a minute-boundary timestamp, `pw-13` the virtualised probe), which marks taxonomy gaps rather than wording defects; those are not addressed here.

## 3. Rules that were missing, ambiguous, or distracting in the 2.0.0 skill text

1. **Missing: an F1 / F14 / F11 tie-break.** `SKILL.md` Phase 2 and `classification-procedure.md` give a detailed F1-versus-F7 procedure, an F15-versus-F14 paragraph, and (Cypress) an F3/F11 intercept-ordering rule, but no rule for the boundary that produced 8 of 11 real-fix disputes. The F1 row is symptom-based (`TimeoutError`, near timeout, passes on retry) and the F14 and F11 rows are mechanism-based, so a case showing both a retry pass and a render race qualifies for two codes.
2. **Ambiguous: F11's scope.** "Promise.all order, parallel race" (Playwright) reads as either a test-side ordering assumption or any concurrency effect. Mapper A used it for a product fetch race (`pw-04`), a backend write that completes after its 200 (`pw-08`), a single intercept among several initial requests (`cy-04`), and parallel-worker contention (`pw-06`); mapper B used F1 or F7 for the same cases. The row does not say that cross-test or cross-worker interference belongs to F7 regardless of symptom.
3. **Distracting: F5's "wrong state remains".** The row describes an outcome, not a mechanism, so a click that missed its target and therefore changed nothing (`cy-06`) reads as a "missing Then". The skill arm then reported the absent verification rather than the reason the action missed.
4. **Missing: a shape rule for the Root Cause line.** The output contract says "Root Cause: one-sentence explanation". Statements that restated the category ("raced a transient listbox") or the timeline ("closed at 240 ms, after the scroll") were graded as partial by a judge comparing against the maintainers' cause-and-effect sentence. A rule that the sentence must name the trigger, the mechanism, and the observed effect would have cost nothing and matches how maintainers write fixes.
5. **Not a finding here: always-loaded size.** The 43 KB zero-tool prompt did not hurt accuracy in v2 (skill tied or beat legend), so no evidence from v2 supports cutting references; token cost (about 10x the free arm) is a cost finding, not a diagnosis finding. Stage 2 leaves the reference layout alone because the CI debugger contracts pin the reference routing and because the arms must stay comparable.

These findings are from 26 selected public cases and one host; they motivate a small wording change to be tested on fresh cases, not a conclusion about the taxonomy.
